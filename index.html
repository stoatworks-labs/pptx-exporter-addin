Advanced Exporter for PowerPointAn add-in, from Stoatworks Labs ·

PowerPoint will save a deck as a PDF, a picture per slide or a video, and it asks you remarkably little about any of them. This add-in does all of those with the options left in — size, resolution, quality, slide range, letterboxing — and then carries on into the formats PowerPoint never got round to: a self-contained HTML5 presentation, reveal.js, LaTeX Beamer, Markdown and Marp, handouts, notes pages, and a few more.

Download for Mac Download for Windows

A small installer that adds the add-in to PowerPoint for your account, with no admin password. The add-in itself runs from this site, so it keeps itself up to date, and nothing about your deck leaves your machine. PowerPoint on the web: manifest.xml. Where it has been tried

Built and tested against PowerPoint for Mac 16.112, the Mac installer and uninstaller included. The Windows installer has been tried on Windows and works. The formats haven't been checked one by one in PowerPoint for Windows, and PowerPoint on the web hasn't been tried. If you try either, say how it went.

What it exports

ImagesPNG, JPEG, WebP, AVIF, TIFF (single or multi-page, LZW/Deflate/PackBits), BMP, TGA, GIF, SVG with live links, and a contact sheet. Any size from a thumbnail to 8K, or a print resolution in dpi, letterboxed or cropped to an exact raster when the deck's shape doesn't match the screen's.
DocumentsPowerPoint's own vector PDF, trimmed to a range or split per slide; a picture PDF that keeps hidden slides and links; handouts 1, 2, 3 (with lines), 4, 6 or 9 to a page; notes pages; a Word notes document; OpenDocument.
WebA self-contained HTML5 presentation with transitions, a presenter view, clickable links and the deck's embedded video; reveal.js; a scrolling page that reads well on a phone.
VideoMP4 (H.264, HEVC, AV1) and WebM (VP9, VP8, AV1) at any frame rate, timed from the deck's rehearsed timings or your own, with captions from the notes; animated GIF.
TextLaTeX Beamer — the slides as drawn, or rebuilt as editable LaTeX — or a LaTeX article with the notes; Markdown, Marp and Pandoc/Quarto; an outline PowerPoint reads back in; the speaker notes alone; CSV; JSON.
PowerPointA cleaned copy (notes, comments, hidden slides or personal details removed), as a show or a template; one file per slide; a flattened deck of pictures that plays the same on a machine without your fonts.

Install

PowerPoint for Mac

  1. Open Advanced-Exporter-for-PowerPoint.pkg. It's signed and notarised, installs for your account only (no admin password), and asks to quit PowerPoint if it's open. PowerPoint has to have been opened at least once on this Mac.
  2. When macOS asks whether Installer may access data from other apps, choose Allow. That's the installer reaching PowerPoint's add-ins folder, which lives inside PowerPoint's own storage. The uninstaller asks the same.
  3. Open PowerPoint and a deck, then Home ▸ Add-ins ▸ Developer Add-ins ▸ Advanced Exporter. That also puts an Advanced Exporter button on the Home tab until PowerPoint quits; next time, start from Home ▸ Add-ins again.
  4. To remove it, open Uninstall-Advanced-Exporter-for-PowerPoint.pkg.

PowerPoint for Windows

  1. Close PowerPoint and run Advanced-Exporter-for-PowerPoint-Setup.exe. It installs for your account only, with no administrator prompt. It isn't code-signed yet, so SmartScreen will say it doesn't recognise it: More info ▸ Run anyway.
  2. Open PowerPoint and a deck. Advanced Exporter has a button on the Home tab; if it isn't there, choose Home ▸ Add-ins and pick it from your add-ins.
  3. To remove it, uninstall Advanced Exporter for PowerPoint from Settings ▸ Apps ▸ Installed apps.

PowerPoint on the web

  1. Download manifest.xml.
  2. Home ▸ Add-ins ▸ More Add-ins (or Insert ▸ Add-ins), then My Add-ins ▸ Upload My Add-in, and pick it. The pane opens from the Home tab. The same works in Microsoft 365 on Windows if you'd rather not run an installer.

All the installers do is put manifest.xml where PowerPoint looks for add-ins: on a Mac, ~/Library/Containers/com.microsoft.Powerpoint/Data/Documents/wef/; on Windows, a registry value under HKCU\Software\Microsoft\Office\16.0\Wef\Developer. Doing it by hand works just as well, and Font Manager for PowerPoint installs beside it without either touching the other.

The honest limits

Animations don't come along. Every picture-based format shows each slide as it looks once its builds have finished. Video and GIF are slides with transitions between them, not a recording of the slide show.

Hidden slides aren't in PowerPoint's PDF. The vector PDF formats say so when your range includes one; the picture-based ones draw it anyway.

It needs a reasonably recent PowerPoint for the best pictures. PowerPoint draws each slide for the add-in on version 16.96 or later on a Mac, and Microsoft 365 version 2504 or later on Windows. Older versions (and Office LTSC) fall back to rasterising PowerPoint's PDF, which works but leaves hidden slides out.
